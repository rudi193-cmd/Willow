# The Second-Watershed Limit — Proposed Charter Clause

*Prepared for operator review. Draft of a proposed clause; does NOT edit
`CONSTITUTION.md`. Source: Ivan Illich, *Tools for Conviviality* (1973) +
*Deschooling Society* (1971), via the vault brief `illich-against-the-fleet-2026-07-14`
(session 7a5806cc). Companion to `egress-membrane-constitutional-map.md`.*

---

## The finding this encodes

Illich, sent to read the whole enterprise, **blessed the ends and indicted the
means** — wherever a tool becomes *durable, indispensable, or definition-owning*,
it turns into the thing he spent a life warning against. Two arms of the fleet
passed his audit clean: the **friction floor** (the deliberate cost that keeps the
tool from being frictionlessly over-used) and **community ownership** (the user
holds the tool, not the reverse). His one prescription for the rest: make the limit
*explicit* — a charter clause, not a good intention.

The **second watershed** is Illich's exact concept: the point at which a tool
crosses from *serving* a human purpose to *dominating* it — where more of the tool
produces less of the good it was for. School past its second watershed makes people
feel unable to learn without it; medicine past it manufactures the sickness it
treats. The clause below is a tripwire the fleet must not cross.

## Proposed clause (candidate text, unratified)

> **III.6 — The Second-Watershed Limit.** *(CONST-III-6, proposed)*
> A tool of the fleet may serve a human capacity; it may never replace it. No fleet
> system may cross the **second watershed** — the point past which its growth
> diminishes the very good it exists for — and three specific crossings are
> forbidden by name:
>
> 1. **Indispensability.** No fleet tool may make its user *unable* to accomplish
>    the tool's purpose without it. The user's autonomous competence must survive
>    the tool's removal. (For UTETY: a child who used the system must be *more* able
>    to learn unaided, never less. The measure is competence retained when the tool
>    is switched off, tested — not assumed.)
> 2. **Definition-ownership.** No fleet tool may become the *sole authority* on what
>    counts as correct, competent, or true within its domain. The canon tiers
>    (Article IV) and an external source of record must always be reachable *around*
>    the tool, not only *through* it. A tool that owns its own grading owns its own
>    truth (§0.1).
> 3. **Frictionless durability.** The **friction floor** (a deliberate, non-zero cost
>    of use) is strengthen-only and may not be optimized away in the name of
>    convenience. A tool too cheap to question is used past the point of good.
>
> Crossing, or approaching, any of the three is a Constitutional Review matter
> (Article XI): the tool is suspended pending human ruling, not throttled by runtime
> heuristic. The limit is the operator's to set numerically; the *existence* of the
> limit is not optional.

## Why III.6, and why strengthen-only

- It lives in **Article III (Reach)** because it is a bound on *how far a tool may
  reach into the human it serves* — the axis beyond files and networks: reach into
  competence and dependence. It is the human-capacity analogue of III.5 (reach over
  content).
- It borrows the **eternity-clause posture** (strengthen-only, like III.5's
  redaction floor) without living in Article 0: the *limit* may be tightened, never
  loosened, because a second-watershed bound a product manager can relax under
  growth pressure is not a bound.
- It converts Illich's diagnosis into the fleet's own idiom: indispensability is a
  §0.3-shaped harm (the tool extends its hold), definition-ownership is a §0.1-shaped
  harm (the witness becomes the actor), frictionless durability is the failure the
  friction floor already answers.

## Open parameters for the operator

- The **competence-retained** test for UTETY: what fraction of unaided capability
  must survive tool removal, measured how, how often. (Proposed: a periodic
  tool-off assessment; number is the operator's.)
- The **friction-floor minimum** per surface (already partly set elsewhere; this
  clause makes it non-optimizable, does not set its value).
- Whether III.6 is fleet-general (current draft) or scoped to the education arm.

## Non-goals

- This edits nothing. It is a proposed clause for Article VIII process.
- It does not set the numbers; it establishes that the numbers exist and are
  strengthen-only.

*Draft — unratified. The ends were blessed; this is the limit on the means.*
