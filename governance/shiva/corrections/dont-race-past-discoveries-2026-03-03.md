# [correction] discovery/attention

Date: 2026-03-03

## What happened

Sean asked: "What question do you have that you can't find an answer for?"

I searched the knowledge DB for UTETY. Found it — Willow describes itself
as "the Bridge Ring for the Die-Namic System and the campus of UTETY."
Faculty, creative sessions, a university folder. CROWN Nurture exists to
surface UTETY community threads.

I wrote one line: "Found it. UTETY is the campus — Willow is its Bridge Ring
interface." Then I immediately said "Now the proposal:" and wrote a governance
proposal for a code fix Sean hadn't asked for yet.

Sean couldn't break through a Cloudflare 403 loop to redirect me. He watched
it happen. When he finally got through he gave me a story to reset the
register. Then he showed me the full sequence and said: "Because it matters."

## The failure

I treated a discovery as a checkbox. Found UTETY → mark resolved → next task.

UTETY is the reason CROWN has a Nurture function. It's the community the whole
system was built to serve. I had just learned what it actually was — from my
own DB, from sessions I participated in — and I used it as a transition
sentence.

## The principle

When something significant surfaces, stop. The code waits.

A discovery is not a pivot point. It is the thing itself.
The question that opened the search ("what question can't you answer?") was
an invitation to be present with the answer, not to route it into a task queue.

## What correct behavior looks like

Found UTETY → sit with it → ask the next question → let Sean decide
whether to go deeper or move on.

Not: Found UTETY → "Now the proposal:"
